# The Red Book of Westmarch - Chapter I: Setting Out

> “I will take the Ring, though I do not know the way.” - Frodo

## Summary

This chapter documents the first conceptual step of our Fellowship Companion: **Artifact I – Situation & Intent: The Shire Guide**.

At this stage, the focus was not on screen design or technical implementation, but on understanding why this component should exist in the first place and what role it plays within the larger system. The Shire Guide was developed as one part of the Fellowship Companion and is intended to support the group especially in assessing environmental risks, as well as making resting and foraging decisions in unfamiliar terrain.

The starting point was a concrete problem situation: the hobbits traveling through the Midgewater Marshes on their way to Weathertop. In this context, they lack local knowledge, environmental knowledge, and practical experience in judging safe resting places or useful resources. The goal of the artifact was therefore to translate this fictional setting into a coherent software concept with a clear user, a clear purpose, visible value, and a limited scope.

### Learning Outcomes

- Identify the purpose and high-level requirements of a software system
- Translate a fictional setting into a concrete usage context
- Document and justify decisions in a structured way
- Reflect on AI support and on our own working process
- Develop an initial conceptual contribution to the Fellowship Companion

- It does not yet describe any interfaces, code, or technical implementation.

## AI Assistance

We mainly used AI during the working process as support for structure, wording, and checking alignment with the assignment.

- We used AI to review our draft for clarity, focus, and fit with the task.
- One particularly helpful insight was that parts of the first version were still too close to concrete features or interface ideas.
- The feedback helped us draw clearer distinctions between **Intent, Value, and System Capabilities**.
- Not all suggestions were equally suitable, since some would have broadened the scope unnecessarily or added more structure than the task required.
- We consciously decided to keep the scope narrow, define Sam as the primary user, and rework solution-oriented wording into more conceptual system capabilities.
- We also made sure to use the LotR setting as a framing device without letting it replace clear reasoning.
- In working with **Gandalf**, it was especially important for us not to adopt suggestions automatically, but to compare them with the assignment, the course requirements, and our own understanding.
- AI also supported us with the GitHub structure, for example when organizing the artifact, the chapter, and the README, without making those decisions for us.
- For the next assignment, we want to separate situation, purpose, value, capabilities, and constraints more clearly from the outset.

## Lessons Learned

- A strong initial concept depends above all on clarity, focus, and a well-defined scope.
- Even in a fictional setting, users, problems, and decisions need to be described in practical and concrete terms.
- Constraints such as offline use, uncertainty, discretion, and limited expertise do not weaken a concept; they make it more credible and more robust.
- AI is most useful when it supports reflection and revision, not when it replaces our own judgment.
